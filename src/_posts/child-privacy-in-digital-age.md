Every day, millions of children around the globe pick up tablets and smartphones to play learning games. Yet beneath the surface of many children's applications lies an ecosystem of third-party tracking SDKs, behavioral profiling networks, and targeted advertising engines.

At Bihobol, we believe this model is deeply flawed. Here is why child privacy must come first.

---

## 1. The Perils of Advertising in Children’s Software

Children under the age of 10 lack the cognitive defense mechanisms to distinguish between educational content and persuasive marketing. When an app presents an advertisement—whether a banner, an interstitial video, or a rewarded commercial—it breaks learning immersion and exploits vulnerable attention spans.

Worse, ad SDKs frequently attempt to track user behavior across apps and devices, compiling persistent profiles on minors.

---

## 2. What COPPA and GDPR-K Demand

The **Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA)** in the United States and **GDPR-K** in the European Union establish strict legal standards:
* Operators may not collect personal information from children without verified parental consent.
* Persistent identifiers used for behavioral advertising are explicitly classified as personal information.
* Apps directed to children must provide clear, concise privacy policies outlining all data practices.

At Bihobol, we go beyond mere baseline legal compliance: **we simply collect zero data.**

---

## 3. The Power of Offline Architecture

The safest data is the data that is never collected. By engineering our games (such as Math Adventure) to function entirely offline without server accounts, we eliminate data breaches, credential leaks, and tracking risks at the architectural level.

We encourage all developers in the kids space to adopt these privacy-first principles.
